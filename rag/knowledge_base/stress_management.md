# Stress Management & Autonomic Regulation

## Allostatic Load and Stress Neurobiology
Stress is a physiological and behavioral response to demands that test our homeostatic capacity. While acute stress enhances alertness via catecholamines (epinephrine, norepinephrine), chronic stress results in sustained elevated cortisol, prefrontal cortical atrophy, and impaired immune function.

## Evidence-Based Coping Strategies

### The Dual-Axis Regulation Model
1. **Top-Down Regulation (Cognitive)**:
   - Appraisal Theory: Distinguishing between threat appraisals (where demands exceed resources) and challenge appraisals (where demands are met with agency).
   - Problem-focused coping for controllable stressors vs. emotion-focused coping for uncontrollable existential or situational stressors.

2. **Bottom-Up Regulation (Somatic)**:
   - Resonant Frequency Breathing (approx. 5.5 to 6 breaths per minute) balances Heart Rate Variability (HRV) and baroreflex sensitivity.
   - Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR) and Yoga Nidra: 10–20 minute resting states shown to replenish striatal dopamine and reduce autonomic sympathetic tone.

### Social Buffering & Oxytocinergic Support
- Mammalian nervous systems coregulate through prosocial contact.
- Authentic verbal disclosure of emotional states to an empathic listener rapidly attenuates amygdala reactivity.
