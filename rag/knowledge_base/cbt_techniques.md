# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Foundations

## Core Principles
Cognitive Behavioral Therapy operates on the empirically validated model that psychological distress is heavily mediated not by external events themselves, but by the cognitive interpretations and appraisals placed upon those events.

### The Cognitive Triangle
- **Thoughts**: What we whisper to ourselves internally. Example: *"I will definitely fail this test."*
- **Emotions**: What we physically feel emotionally. Example: Anxiety, dread, shame.
- **Behaviors**: How we respond. Example: Avoidance, procrastination, social withdrawal.

## Common Cognitive Distortions
1. **Catastrophizing**: Anticipating the absolute worst possible outcome without evaluating realistic probabilities.
2. **All-or-Nothing Thinking (Black-and-White)**: Viewing situations in extreme categories. *"If I am not completely perfect, I am a failure."*
3. **Emotional Reasoning**: Assuming that because an emotion is intense, it reflects factual reality. *"I feel anxious, therefore danger is imminent."*
4. **Mind Reading**: Assuming one knows what others are negatively thinking about them without direct evidence.
5. **Overgeneralization**: Taking a single negative event as a universal pattern. *"Nothing ever works out for me."*

## Evidence-Based Interventions
### The Thought Record (Three C's Protocol)
1. **Catch**: Recognize automatic negative thoughts when emotional intensity shifts.
2. **Check**: Evaluate objective evidence supporting the thought versus objective evidence contradicting it. Ask: *"What would I tell a compassionate friend in this exact situation?"*
3. **Change**: Formulate an evidence-grounded, balanced alternative thought.

### Behavioral Activation
When depressed or lethargic, natural motivation drops. Behavioral activation reverses this negative feedback loop through graded task assignment:
- Breaking intimidating projects into 5-minute micro-steps.
- Focusing on values-aligned activities rather than awaiting spontaneous motivation.
